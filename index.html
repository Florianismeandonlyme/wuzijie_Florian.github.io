WU Zi-Jie © 2026

Empirical Legal Studies · Law and Economics · Computational Social Science

I study how courts process pressure, information, and institutional change.

My work uses large-scale Chinese judicial decisions, patent documents, spatial data, and text-as-data methods to study judicial behavior, legal enforcement, crime, and innovation. I am especially interested in how digital legal infrastructure, guiding cases, and AI-assisted retrieval may reshape courts as knowledge-producing organizations.

Research Interests

  • Judicial decision-making under workload pressure and institutional reform.
  • Digital courts, case retrieval, legal knowledge diffusion, and judicial human capital.
  • Economics of crime, public safety infrastructure, and law enforcement.
  • Innovation, contract enforcement, industrial policy, and urban amenities.
  • Large-scale legal text analysis with Python, Stata, NLP, and spatial data.

Working Papers

  1. Judicial Decisions Under Pressure: Evidence from the Chinese Judiciary

    Working paper · 3.3 million Chinese civil appellate decisions, 2013-2021

    I study whether caseload pressure changes appellate decision-making. A doubling of appellate caseload increases the probability that an appellate court upholds the first-instance judgment and reduces legal citations while increasing textual similarity between first- and second-instance reasoning.

    paper

  2. When the Sun Goes Down: Darkness, Crime, and the Substitutability of Public Infrastructure and Services

    Working paper · Chinese version won Third Prize, Fayan Lighthouse National Judicial Big Data Essay Contest

    Using hour-level criminal records, I estimate how sunlight affects criminal activity in China. Darkness increases crime, especially robbery and theft, while nightlights and frontline police force attenuate this effect.

    paper

  3. Judicial Reform and Breakthrough Innovation: Evidence from China

    Economic Change and Restructuring, under review

    I examine China's 2020 complex-simple civil case diversion reform. The reform increased breakthrough patents by improving civil dispute resolution speed, easing financing constraints, and raising R&D spending.

    ssrn

  4. Central-Local Industrial Policy Coordination, Local Comparative Advantage, and High-Quality Innovation

    With Gang Chen, Chao Lei, Siyu Wang, and Zhiting Wu · The Journal of World Economy, under review

    I constructed city-level breakthrough innovation measures using Chinese patent text similarity, following Kelly et al. (2021), to study how central-local policy alignment affects innovation.

  5. Urban Spatial Aesthetics, Innovation Factor Allocation, and Breakthrough Innovation

    With Gang Chen and Chong Wang · China Economic Quarterly, under review

    I collected around five million Chinese art auction records to construct historical aesthetic endowment instruments for studying urban street aesthetics and breakthrough innovation.
