Hi, I'm **Wu Zi-Jie (Florian)**, an MPhil candidate in Law and Economics at the Shanghai University of International Business and Economics (SUIBE). 

My research explores the intersection of **Empirical Legal Studies, Law and Economics, Economics of Crime, and Judicial Politics**. I am particularly interested in how institutional reforms, legal enforcement, and environmental factors shape economic behavior and judicial decision-making in China.

Methodologically, I am passionate about applying **advanced econometrics** (Causal Inference, DiD, IV, Double Machine Learning) and **computational methods** to complex social science questions. My work heavily utilizes **Natural Language Processing (NLP) / Text-as-Data** to parse millions of judicial verdicts and patent documents, as well as **Computer Vision and Spatial Data Analysis** to quantify urban environments.

## Recent Research Highlights

* **Judicial Reform and Innovation:** Investigating how China's complex-simple civil case diversion reform accelerates contract enforcement, relaxes corporate financial constraints, and promotes breakthrough innovation.
* **Judicial Decision Under Pressure:** Using NLP techniques (Sentence-BERT, Lempel-Ziv complexity) and peer caseload as an instrumental variable to examine how workload pressure affects judicial bias and transparency in appellate courts.
* **Darkness and Crime:** Exploiting exogenous variation in sunset times across China to identify the causal effect of darkness on criminal activity, and the substitutability of public infrastructure (streetlights, police).
* **Urban Space Aesthetics:** Using large-scale street view imagery and vision-language models (StreetCLIP) to show how urban aesthetics attract high-end talent and drive breakthrough innovation.
