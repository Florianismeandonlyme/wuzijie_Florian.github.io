Hi, I'm **WU Zi-Jie (Florian)**, an MPhil candidate in Law and Economics at the Shanghai University of International Business and Economics (SUIBE). 

My research explores the intersection of **Empirical Legal Studies, Law and Economics, Economics of Crime, and Judicial Politics**. I am particularly interested in how institutional reforms, legal enforcement, and environmental factors shape economic behavior and judicial decision-making in China.

Methodologically, I use **econometrics** and **computational social science** methods to study complex legal and economic questions. My work uses large-scale judicial decisions, patent documents, text-as-data methods, spatial data, and computer vision tools.

## Recent Research Highlights

* **Judicial Reform and Innovation:** Investigating how China's complex-simple civil case diversion reform accelerates contract enforcement, relaxes corporate financial constraints, and promotes breakthrough innovation.
* **Judicial Decisions Under Pressure:** Studying how judge-level workload affects appellate review, legal citations, and textual similarity in Chinese civil appellate decisions.
* **Darkness and Crime:** Exploiting exogenous variation in sunset times across China to identify how darkness affects criminal activity and how public safety infrastructure moderates this effect.
* **Urban Spatial Aesthetics:** Using large-scale street-view imagery and city-level historical aesthetic endowments to study the allocation of innovation factors and breakthrough innovation.
