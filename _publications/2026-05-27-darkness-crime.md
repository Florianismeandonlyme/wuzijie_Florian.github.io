With a novel hour-level criminal records dataset, this article provides the first empirical evidence on how sunlight affects criminal activity in China. Exploiting the exogenous variation in sunset time during a day as a natural experiment, we find that darkness significantly increases criminal activity by approximately 4.5%. Moreover, this effect is entirely driven by robbery and theft, with no significant impact on other impulsive violent offenses. Finally, we document that public infrastructure and service act as an effective substitute for natural light: the criminogenic effect of darkness is significantly attenuated in cities with higher density of more nightlights, more frontline police force, while the road density exaggerated the effect of darkness. Our findings highlight the role of visibility and policing as a binding constraint on criminal behavior and underscore the social value of public safety investments.
