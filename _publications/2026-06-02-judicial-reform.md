China's 2020 complex-simple civil case diversion reform increased breakthrough patents by 24.9 percent, while leaving total and incremental patents statistically unchanged. The paper uses the reform as a quasi-natural experiment that improved the speed of civil dispute resolution in 20 pilot cities. The evidence points to a financial-enforcement channel: treated courts resolve debt-related civil cases faster, exposed firms receive more trade credit, face lower financing constraints, and raise R&D spending. The effect is larger in technologically specialized city-industry cells and in industries with higher litigation intensity. The results show that procedural judicial efficiency matters for innovation because firms need enforceable contracts to finance and organize risky research.
