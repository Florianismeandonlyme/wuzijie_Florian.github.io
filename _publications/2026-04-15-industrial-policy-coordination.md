Against the background of the central government's continued strengthening of industrial guidance and local governments' active implementation of national strategies, how central-local industrial policy coordination affects local innovation is an important question for understanding high-quality development and the construction of a modern industrial system. Based on the texts of central and local government work reports, this paper constructs measures of central and local policy preferences at the industry level and their similarity, and combines these measures with city-industry-level innovation data to examine the effects and mechanisms of local governments' following of central industrial priorities on different types of innovation. The paper finds that, overall, greater alignment between local industrial policy and central guidance is associated with stronger innovation performance, but this positive relationship exhibits significant industrial heterogeneity. In non-advantage industries, local following of central guidance significantly suppresses breakthrough innovation; in advantage industries, such following produces a stronger promoting effect on breakthrough innovation. Mechanism analyses show that, in non-advantage industries, local following suppresses breakthrough innovation by aggravating resource misallocation, whereas in advantage industries it promotes breakthrough innovation by increasing R&D intensity. The paper shows that central-local industrial policy coordination is not a simple matter of "the more consistent, the better"; its economic consequences depend heavily on whether central guidance matches local comparative advantage. Only when comparative advantage is respected and local conditions are taken into account can central guidance be transformed into sustained momentum for high-quality innovation and effective investment.

My main contribution: Following the text-analysis method proposed by Kelly et al. (2021, <i>AER: Insights</i>), I used Chinese patent application data from 2005 to 2025 to calculate each patent's forward and backward similarity, evaluate its breakthrough level, and construct city-level breakthrough innovation measures as the core dependent variables of the paper.
