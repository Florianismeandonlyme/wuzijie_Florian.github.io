This paper examines the effect of urban street aesthetics on the share of breakthrough innovation. Street aesthetics are measured using Baidu historical street-view images and MIT's Place Pulse street-view evaluation algorithm, which identifies aesthetic scores through pairwise comparisons. To address endogeneity, the paper further maps modern and contemporary calligraphy and painting art to cities' historical aesthetic endowments and interacts these measures with the textual intensity of urban renewal and landscape governance to construct an instrumental variable. The paper finds that urban street aesthetics significantly increase the share of breakthrough innovation, and this conclusion remains robust after replacing the measure and using instrumental-variable estimation. The effect is concentrated in technology-intensive industries and operates through the agglomeration of high-end talent and the enhancement of autonomous knowledge absorption.

My main contribution: Following Huang et al. (2019, <i>Journal of Financial Research</i>), I used web scraping to collect approximately five million art auction records in China from 1995 to 2024 and constructed the instrumental variable used in this paper based on these data.
